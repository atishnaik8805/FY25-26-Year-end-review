▮ CHAPTER I — The Ascension — May 2025
The year started with a title change. Lead Consultant. No fanfare. No transition period. Just a new label and the same desk.

What the title meant in practice was a shift in how ownership worked. Not waiting to be assigned. Not waiting for a clear brief. Taking things that mattered and making them yours — even when nobody asked.

That instinct shaped everything that followed.
▮ CHAPTER II — DAS Ownership — May to October 2025
The first half of FY26 was DAS. Fully, heavily, completely DAS.

What ownership looked like:
> Procedures, risk publishing microservice, reference copy, checklists — taken on without being asked — not really , Phil knows what i am talking about 😉
> Deep code-level understanding of DAS complexity and architecture
> Documentation created and maintained to improve maintainability across the team
> Realistic expectations set with clients and peers — no over-promising

The AI initiative nobody assigned:
Alongside the core DAS work, a self-driven exploration began — how do you actually use AI effectively in a real development workflow? Not theory. Not demos. Actual day-to-day integration.

> Experimented with AI in development, unit testing, and automation
> Created spec files as structured guidelines for getting consistent, high-quality output from AI models
> Explored what it takes to extract maximum value from AI tools when the models were still finding their footing
> This was early-phase AI — promising, inconsistent, worth figuring out

This work became the foundation. The Prompt Engineering course, the AI learning paths, the team adoption strategy — all of it grew from this quiet, self-initiated phase.
▮ CHAPTER III — Mentoring & The Empower Shift — Throughout FY26
Mentoring was not a side task. It ran in parallel with everything else, every week.

New Empowers — first principles first:
> Weekly connect sessions focused on how to think, not just what to do
> Architectural logic, first-principles reasoning, ownership mindset
> The goal: engineers who analyse and own their solutions rather than follow a roadmap

The deliberate leadership shift with previous Empowers:
The PO Portfolio Dashboard project was used intentionally as a proving ground. The previous Empower cohort was pushed to move from implementing solutions to guiding and overseeing them. The new cohort became the implementers. The old cohort became the mentors.

> Old Empowers: shifted from doing to leading and reviewing
> New Empowers: took on implementation with guidance from peers, not just seniors
> The result: a mentorship chain that didn't depend entirely on Atish to function

This was deliberate. Leadership that replicates itself is more valuable than leadership that is required.
▮ CHAPTER IV — AI Unlocks — October 2025 Onwards
Around October 2025, something shifted. The models crossed a threshold.

Code became cheap. Execution became fast. The only thing that mattered was what to build and how effectively you used the tools.

The workflow evolved with it:
> Agentic AI exploration — moving beyond prompts to agent-based systems
> MCP (Model Context Protocol) — explored integrations with Jira and GitHub to improve development workflows directly
> Token efficiency — actively worked on reducing token consumption. Explored approaches like Caveman (a deeply personal area of interest — doing more with less context)
> Spec Driven Development — drove adoption across the team; specs as the structured interface between human intent and AI output

What got shipped during this phase:
> QA Gen Tool — AI-powered test case and bug management through Jira API (Python + AI-Based System Design)
> Interview Toolkit — built and deployed for the Digital Empower 2026-27 recruitment drive
> This Year End Review site — vibe-coded. You are inside it right now.

The constraint was never the code. It was always the ideas.
▮ CHAPTER V — Ideas in Progress — The Thoughts That Followed
When execution gets fast, ideas multiply. Not all of them land. That's the point.

Ideas being explored or in progress:

> DOM-aware automation via MCP
    Fetch live UI DOM through MCP so that writing automation tests no longer requires manually copying DOM structure into prompts. Similar in spirit to what Playwright MCP does — but the goal is to go further: make it more efficient, produce higher quality results, and deliver more value than the existing approach.

> Virtual Scrum Master
    An AI-assisted system to reduce the operational burden on Scrum Masters — automating routine facilitation, tracking, and reporting so SMs can focus on what actually needs human judgment.

> Deeper token efficiency work (Caveman approach)
    Continuing to find ways to do more with less context. Smaller, more targeted prompts. Leaner workflows. The less you give an AI model unnecessarily, the more reliable and cheaper the output.

These are not finished products. They are directions. The fact that they exist is the relevant data point — fast execution creates space for more thinking, and more thinking creates more directions worth pursuing.
▮ CHAPTER VI — Leading by Example — The Full Picture
The Lead Consultant role was never going to be about title. It was always going to be about what you do when the camera is off.

What that looked like across FY26:
> DAS: took ownership of the hard, unglamorous parts — and made them well-understood and documented
> AI: initiated and drove adoption before it was a priority, built the learning infrastructure, then shipped real tools when the models were ready
> Mentoring: ran weekly sessions, built a self-replicating mentorship chain via the Empower cohort model
> Spec Driven Development: explored, adopted, shared, pushed for org-wide uptake
> AI Architecture: explored patterns and shared findings to help the team make better technical decisions
> Non-DAS projects: QA Gen, Interview Toolkit, Year End Site — shipped with AI, not despite the DAS workload but in the time that remained

The clearest signal of a lead is not what they do when they are asked. It is what they do when they are not.
FY26 in one line:
Took ownership. Built foundations. Shipped things that didn't exist before. Helped others grow. Ran out of time before running out of ideas. That's the right problem to have.
